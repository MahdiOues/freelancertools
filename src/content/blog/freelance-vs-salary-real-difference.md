When comparing a job offer to freelance work, the headline numbers are misleading. A $100,000 salary is not the same as $100,000 in freelance revenue. The gap comes from taxes, benefits, downtime, and overhead.

## The tax gap

As an employee, your employer pays half your FICA taxes (7.65%). As a freelancer, you pay both halves (15.3%). On $100,000 of income, that is $7,650 more in taxes.

As an employee, your employer withholds taxes from each paycheck. As a freelancer, you must make quarterly estimated payments. Miss one and you face penalties.

## The benefits gap

Employers pay roughly 30% of salary on benefits: health insurance, retirement contributions, paid time off, sick leave, and workers compensation. As a freelancer, you pay 100% of these costs. Health insurance alone can cost $500-1,500 per month for a family plan.

## The downtime gap

Employees get paid for 52 weeks per year. Freelancers bill for 20-30 hours per week and take unpaid time off for sick days, holidays, and vacations. A freelancer who bills 1,200 hours per year at $100/hour earns $120,000 in revenue, but after expenses and taxes, the take-home is roughly equivalent to a $70,000 salary.

## The true hourly rate formula

To find the freelance equivalent of a salary:
1. Multiply salary by 1.3 (benefits + employer taxes)
2. Add business expenses
3. Divide by billable hours

A $100,000 salary: $100,000 × 1.3 = $130,000. Add $15,000 in expenses = $145,000. Divide by 1,200 hours = $121/hour.

## Frequently asked questions

<details><summary>Is freelancing more profitable than a salary?</summary>
Freelancing can be more profitable, but only if you account for the hidden costs. A freelancer charging $100/hour working 1,200 billable hours earns $120,000 gross — but after taxes, expenses, unpaid time off, and health insurance, the net is often comparable to a $70,000-80,000 salary.
</details>

<details><summary>How do benefits compare between freelance and salary?</summary>
As a salaried employee, benefits (health insurance, retirement matching, paid time off, training) typically add 25-35% to your base compensation. A $70,000 salary might be worth $90,000+ with benefits. Freelancers must fund these themselves, making health insurance ($300-800/month) and paid time off significant costs.
</details>

<details><summary>What is the true hourly rate difference between freelancing and employment?</summary>
To match a $70,000 salary with benefits, a freelancer needs to earn roughly $90,000-100,000 in gross revenue. At 1,200 billable hours per year, that means an hourly rate of $75-83/hour just to break even with the salaried position, not accounting for business growth or profit.
</details>

<details><summary>How much more should a freelancer charge than an employee?</summary>
Freelancers should typically charge 1.5-2.5x the equivalent hourly salary rate. If you earned $35/hour as an employee ($70,000/year), your freelance rate should be $70-105/hour. This multiplier covers taxes, benefits, downtime, overhead, and business development costs.
</details>

<details><summary>What are the hidden costs of freelancing?</summary>
The biggest hidden costs are unpaid time off (2-4 weeks/year), slow months with no income, health insurance ($300-800/month), retirement savings (no employer match), software and equipment ($2,000-5,000/year), professional development, accounting fees, and self-employment tax (15.3% vs 7.65% as an employee).
</details>
